## What the photos show

- **Bars.** A 5-octave concert marimba (C2 to C7) has 61 bars; 4.3-octave instruments (A2 to C7) have 52. The model
  uses 61: 36 naturals and 25 accidentals.
- **Taper.** Bars get shorter and narrower from bass (left, seen from the player) to treble (right). The longest bass
  bar is about 2.5 times the length of the shortest treble bar and about 1.6 times its width. Bass bars are also
  thicker.
- **Two rows.** Naturals sit in the front row (player side), accidentals in the back row, raised a little above the
  naturals and grouped 2 and 3 like a piano keyboard (no accidental between E-F and B-C).
- **Bar colour.** Rosewood: a warm reddish brown (about RGB 150, 70, 45) with darker grain; neighbouring bars differ
  slightly in shade.
- **Resonators.** One tube hangs under each bar, the tube length tapering with the bars: the bass tubes reach close
  to the floor, the top-octave tubes are short stubs. Under the treble half the tubes' bottoms trace a smooth curve
  (Marimba One photo). The longest bass tubes double back or end in rounded U bends (Antonko photo). Tubes are black,
  silver or gold; the model uses brass/gold, which reads better on a dark ring.
- **Frame.** A wooden end board stands at each end; the front rail runs straight along the player's side and the back
  rail angles in towards the treble, so seen from above the frame is a trapezoid (deeper at the bass). Under each end
  board a metal end frame (two legs and a foot) stands on two wheels; a long black stretcher bar runs between the two
  end frames at about half height, with a diagonal brace.
- **Proportions** (5-octave concert instrument): length about 2.5 m, depth about 1.0 m at the bass end and 0.6 m at
  the treble end, playing height about 0.9 m. Length : depth : height about 2.5 : 1 : 0.9. The model shortens the
  length a little (about 2.2 : 0.9 : 1) so it still reads at a small radius.
